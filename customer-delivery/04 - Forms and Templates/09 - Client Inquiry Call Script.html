Client Inquiry Call Script

Guide Inbound Calls Toward the Right Next Step

Use this script as a customizable intake guide for inbound calls. Confirm that every service, availability statement, assessment offer, and response time you use is actually true for your agency.

Step 1 — Opening

“Thank you for calling [Agency Name], this is [Your Name]. How can I help you and your family today?”
Let the caller talk. Listen for who needs care, why they're calling now, and any urgency (hospital discharge, recent fall, caregiver burnout, etc.) before moving to discovery questions.

Step 2 — Discovery Questions

  • Who is the care for — you, or a family member? What is their name and age?
  • What's prompting the call right now?
  • What kind of help is needed — personal care, companionship, meal prep, transportation?
  • How many hours or days per week are you thinking?
  • Where are they located (city/zip)?
  • Is this private pay, long-term care insurance, or another payment source?
  • How soon would you like care to start?

Step 3 — Identify the Situation

Click the option that best matches the caller, then use the talking points that appear.

Talking Points — Hospital / Rehab Discharge

“I'm sorry to hear about the hospital stay — a smooth transition home makes a real difference in recovery. We work closely with discharge planners and can often start care within 24–48 hours. Do you know the discharge date yet, and has a doctor recommended any specific level of support?”

Flag as urgent for the scheduling coordinator. Prioritize a same-day or next-day in-home assessment and confirm caregiver availability before ending the call.

Talking Points — Dementia / Memory Care Concern

“That sounds like it's been a difficult adjustment for your family. Our caregivers receive specific training in dementia care — communication techniques, redirection, and keeping a safe, calm routine. Can you tell me a bit about a typical day, and what's been the hardest part recently?”

Note any wandering, sundowning, or safety incidents mentioned. This affects which caregivers are matched to the case.

Talking Points — Family Caregiver Burnout

“It sounds like you've been carrying a lot on your own — that's exactly what respite care is for. Even a few hours a week can make a big difference. What would be most helpful to take off your plate first?”

Gently introduce respite care and companion care packages. Avoid overwhelming with pricing this early — focus on relief and flexibility.

Talking Points — Price-Shopping / Comparing Agencies

“Happy to walk you through our rates. Before I do, can I ask what's most important to you in choosing an agency — is it cost, caregiver consistency, availability, or something else?”

Answer the pricing question directly and honestly, but pivot back to differentiators: caregiver screening, supervision, and consistency of caregiver assignment. Don't discount without manager approval.

Talking Points — General Inquiry

“Happy to answer your questions. Let me first understand the kind of support you are considering so I can explain the services our agency actually offers and whether we may be a good fit.”

Continue with the standard discovery questions above and move toward scheduling an assessment.

Step 4 — Close the Call

“Based on what you have shared, the next step in our agency's intake process is [assessment / consultation / follow-up]. I can offer [two verified times or next-step options] — which works better for you?”
